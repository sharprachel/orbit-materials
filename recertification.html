MATERIAL RECERTIFICATION GUIDE
Explore how salvaged building components can be received, assessed, recertified, stored, and prepared for their next life.

Brick, Stone & Masonry
01
Delivery
Brick arrives from demolition sites in mixed condition. Material is unloaded manually or by forklift and sorted for initial handling.
02
Material Analysis
Bricks are inspected for cracks, mortar residue, chipping, and general condition. Full reusable units are separated from damaged material.
03
Recertification
Mortar is removed manually or mechanically. Structural reuse may require testing or engineering review depending on application.
04
Storage and Reuse
Cleaned bricks are palletized, sorted by type and quality, and stored for resale or reuse in new construction.
Dimensional Lumber
01
Delivery
Recovered dimensional lumber arrives from residential demolition and is unloaded as nailed framing assemblies.
02
Material Analysis
Boards are checked for warping, rot, splitting, fasteners, and coating damage. Reusable pieces are separated from compromised members.
03
Recertification
Lumber is denailed, trimmed, and surface-prepared as needed. If original grade stamps are missing or illegible, structural reuse may require review by a certified grader or engineer.
04
Storage and Reuse
Lumber is sorted by size and condition, stored in dry conditions, and prepared for non-structural reuse or re-graded structural applications.
Window & Frame
01
Delivery
Windows are transported carefully as full assemblies to reduce glass breakage and preserve frame integrity.
02
Material Analysis
Inspection includes frame condition, glazing integrity, seal failure, hardware, and operability.
03
Recertification
Repairs may include refinishing, reglazing, resealing, or hardware replacement. Reuse in primary facade conditions may require testing or engineering review to confirm performance.
04
Storage and Reuse
Windows are stored upright and protected from damage, organized by size and type for resale or reuse.
Door & Frame
01
Delivery
Doors and frames are removed intact where possible and transported with care to preserve surfaces and hardware.
02
Material Analysis
The frame, slab, glazing, hardware, and overall condition are assessed for damage and reuse potential.
03
Recertification
Refinishing, repairs, and hardware replacement may be required. Fire-rated assemblies generally require certified compliance documentation and may not be reusable without it.
04
Storage and Reuse
Doors are stored vertically or in racks, sorted by size and type, and prepared for interior or residential reuse.
Building Envelope Panel
01
Delivery
Panels are removed from building envelope systems and transported in sheets or segments depending on material type.
02
Material Analysis
Inspection includes damage, weathering, fastening systems, and the likelihood of successful reuse.
03
Recertification
Cleaning and minor repair may be possible, but many cladding systems cannot be easily recertified for primary building envelope reuse.
04
Storage and Reuse
Panels are stored flat or stacked depending on material type and prepared for secondary or non-critical reuse where viable.
Heavy Timber
01
Delivery
Large timber members are delivered using heavy equipment and placed with care to avoid splitting or checking.
02
Material Analysis
Members are inspected for cracks, moisture exposure, connection damage, and visible structural defects.
03
Recertification
Timber may require cleaning, cutting, or surface preparation. Structural reuse typically requires engineering review and stamped approval, especially where original documentation is unavailable.
04
Storage and Reuse
Heavy timber is stored flat and protected from moisture for reuse in exposed or structural applications.
Structural Steel
01
Delivery
Steel members are delivered by truck and handled using cranes or forklifts depending on size and weight.
02
Material Analysis
Inspection focuses on corrosion, deformation, weld condition, and connection integrity.
03
Recertification
Steel may require cleaning, trimming, or modification of connections. Structural reuse requires engineering review and stamped approval, and material properties may need verification where documentation is missing.
04
Storage and Reuse
Members are stacked and organized by profile and size for future reuse in structural systems.
